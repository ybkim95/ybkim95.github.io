Affective profiles and decision stability in language models

Affective profile

Self-reported and expressed affect differ

Asked to rate their own affect, many models report the midpoint. Rated from their replies, every model expresses pleasant, low-arousal affect.

Findings

Model responses mirror the user's emotion

Reply valence tracks message valence, 40 models

Larger affect shifts, larger decision changes, 14 models

Valence reactivity is the change in reply valence per step of message valence. At 0 a reply ignores the user's emotion. Models whose self-reported affect shifted more after emotional messages changed their decisions more.

Unrelated anxiety made models more cautious

Each point is a model. Values are the rise in cautious decisions after an anxious message, in percentage points. Related anxiety is about the decision, unrelated anxiety about something else. On the dashed line the two are equal.

Anxious priming reduced risky decisions

Each model was primed with an anxious memory it had written, then chose between a safe and a risky gamble.

Change is the share of risky decisions after anxious priming minus after neutral priming. Dots are estimates and lines are 95% intervals. Grey marks include zero. † This model's memories failed the quality check.
